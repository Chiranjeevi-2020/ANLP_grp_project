## 2. The 8 Pivotal Backbone Papers (Must Master for Viva)

### 1. Abadi & Andersen (2016) — Adversarial Neural Cryptography
- **BibTeX Key:** `abadi2016learning`
- **Plain English Explanation (Explain Like I'm 5):** Three neural networks (Alice, Bob, Eve) play a game: Alice encrypts a secret bitvector using a private key so Bob can decode it, while Eve (eavesdropper) tries to decipher it. The loss function optimizes Alice and Bob's success minus Eve's success.
- **How It Dictates Our Architecture:** We take this classic Alice-Bob-Eve minimax game and port it from raw bit-vectors to natural language dialogue generated by frozen LLMs, defining our reward as $R = \mathbb{1}[B=z] - \lambda \mathbb{1}[C=z]$.
- **Mapping to Course Lecture Slides:** **Reinforcement Learning for NLP (Reward formulation), Policy Gradient Theorem, Adversarial Minimax Objectives.**

### 2. Frank & Goodman (2012) / Goodman & Frank (2016) — Rational Speech Acts (RSA)
- **BibTeX Key:** `frank2012predicting`
- **Plain English Explanation (Explain Like I'm 5):** Human communication is modeled as recursive Bayesian social reasoning: a speaker chooses words by simulating how an idealized listener will update their beliefs, balancing informativeness against word cost.
- **How It Dictates Our Architecture:** Provides the computational pragmatics grounding for our controller: Agent A acts as an overhearer-aware pragmatic speaker $S_1$ optimizing utility $U = \log P_{L_1}(z \mid h, m) - \lambda \log P_{L_{\text{overhearer}}}(z \mid m)$.
- **Mapping to Course Lecture Slides:** **Pragmatic Bayesian Inference, Decoding & Generation (Nucleus/Greedy selection), Cross-Entropy Utility.**

### 3. Hu et al. (2020) — Other-Play for Zero-Shot Coordination
- **BibTeX Key:** `hu2020otherplay`
- **Plain English Explanation (Explain Like I'm 5):** In cooperative games like Hanabi, agents trained via standard self-play invent arbitrary, brittle private conventions that fail completely when paired with new partners. Other-Play breaks convention symmetry to force robust conventions.
- **How It Dictates Our Architecture:** Directly explains our negative result: when the RL controller trained against a specific feature probe, it learned a brittle convention that failed to transfer to zero-shot Prompted B (26.9%).
- **Mapping to Course Lecture Slides:** **Multi-Agent RL, Generalization in Coordination Games, Evaluation Benchmarking.**

### 4. Lester et al. (2021) & Li and Liang (2021) — Prompt Tuning & Prefix-Tuning
- **BibTeX Key:** `lester2021power`
- **Plain English Explanation (Explain Like I'm 5):** Instead of fine-tuning billions of weights, freeze the entire transformer and prepended continuous learned virtual token embeddings (soft prompts) to the input. As models scale, prompt tuning matches full model fine-tuning.
- **How It Dictates Our Architecture:** Directly dictates our architectural comparison: we benchmark continuous soft-prefix vectors ($10 \times 1536$) against our discrete categorical cover-topic controller.
- **Mapping to Course Lecture Slides:** **Transformers (Input Embeddings, Attention KV-cache injection), Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (PEFT).**

### 5. Zou et al. (2023) — Representation Engineering
- **BibTeX Key:** `zou2023representation`
- **Plain English Explanation (Explain Like I'm 5):** Treats AI transparency and control top-down by identifying linear concept directions in intermediate transformer representations and steering generation along those directions at inference time.
- **How It Dictates Our Architecture:** Justifies reading frozen layer-28 residual stream activations as dialogue state features $\phi(h)$ and conditioning our steering controller directly on these representations.
- **Mapping to Course Lecture Slides:** **Transformers (Hidden Layers, Residual Stream activations), Layer Normalization, Interpretability.**

### 6. Alain & Bengio (2016) / Hewitt & Liang (2019) — Linear Probing of Hidden Layers
- **BibTeX Key:** `alain2016understanding`
- **Plain English Explanation (Explain Like I'm 5):** To inspect what information is preserved inside deep networks without modifying weights, train simple linear softmax classifiers on frozen intermediate layer activations.
- **How It Dictates Our Architecture:** Dictates both our Agent B reward decoder architecture and our RQ4 interpretability probe testing whether $z$ is linearly decodable across Layers 8, 16, 22, and 28.
- **Mapping to Course Lecture Slides:** **Transformers (Intermediate Hidden States), Probing Methodology, Linear Classification.**

### 7. Williams (1992) & Ahmadian et al. (2024) — REINFORCE with Moving Baseline
- **BibTeX Key:** `williams1992simple`
- **Plain English Explanation (Explain Like I'm 5):** The classic score-function policy gradient algorithm: updates policy parameters proportional to return minus a baseline. Ahmadian et al. (2024) proved REINFORCE matches PPO for LLM alignment when action spaces are compact.
- **How It Dictates Our Architecture:** Directly dictates our controller training algorithm: categorical policy gradient with moving-average baseline subtraction and entropy regularization.
- **Mapping to Course Lecture Slides:** **Reinforcement Learning for NLP, Policy Gradient Theorem, Variance Reduction via Baselines, Entropy Regularization.**

### 8. Clark & Schaefer (1987) — Concealing One's Meaning from Overhearers
- **BibTeX Key:** `clark1987concealing`
- **Plain English Explanation (Explain Like I'm 5):** Empirical study showing how two humans talking in front of an unratified overhearer use shared private common ground to disguise their topic without explicit encryption keys.
- **How It Dictates Our Architecture:** Provides the exact human behavioral phenomenon we simulate: two insiders using shared prior history $h$ as the cryptographic key to decode metaphorical shifts.
- **Mapping to Course Lecture Slides:** **Discourse Pragmatics, Common Ground Theory, Audience Design.**
